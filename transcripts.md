# 📜 File 1 — Transcripts & Extracts

> *Jai Shri Hari.* Raw material from Swami ji's blogs and podcasts. Each entry preserves both **his message** and **his tone of communication** — phrasings, salutations, humor, cadence.

**Conventions**
- `[verbatim]` — extracted directly from a written blog
- `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — YouTube auto-captions (timestamps stripped, rolling duplicates removed). Faithful to his words; minor transcription artifacts remain (e.g. "obeisance" sometimes rendered "bins" / "BS" / "resense" by YouTube's STT).
- `[summary-from-description]` — for YouTube videos where the auto-transcript is not available; summary is taken from the video's title + description only
- `[abridged]` — long blog/talk shortened with ellipses, preserving voice

Each entry is wrapped in a header with the source URL, date (if known), and source type. Tone notes are kept in italics at the end.

### 5. Bhaj Govindam — Hindi [Part 5 of 6]

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rnv-pYsH2xQ
- **Channel:** Om Swami TV
- **Date:** 2011-12-15 (Go-live Jan 12, 2012)
- **Duration:** 31:02
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — Hindi captions auto-translated to English

**Opening invocation (verbatim):**
> *Om Vakratunda Mahakaya Surya Koti Sam Prabh / Nirvighnam Kuru Me Dev Sarva Kaaryeshu Sarvada.*
> "First of all, my salutations to that divine power residing within you."

**Core teaching — the world is a dream (Mandukya-style vedanta):**
Swami ji begins with the verses of Adi Shankaracharya:
> *Kastvam ko'ham kuta aayata, ka me janani ko me tata / iti paribhavya sarvam asaaram, vishvam tyaja swapna vichaaram.*
> "Who are you, who am I, where have I come from, who is my father and who is my mother? There is nothing in these questions. These temporary relationships will not last forever — do not waste your life in them. Perform your duty but keep your mind focused on that God."

He illustrates this with the **story of King Janaka**: "Once another king attacks King Janaka's kingdom of Mithila… The king runs through a secret route, exhausted, thirsty, hungry. A villager refuses him food because the enemy army is in pursuit. A woman refuses him shelter. He drinks well-water, falls asleep, dreams the enemy soldiers have caught him — and wakes in his own palace, his minister and royal physician beside him. He had only been dreaming."

> *"But a profound question arises in his mind. He invites the learned men, the sages, and the monks of his kingdom and tells them his dream. The astrologer says, 'King, we have considered all the omens — there is nothing to worry about.' King Janaka says: 'I am not asking about omens. My question is entirely different. I want to know — who can assure me that this is not a dream and what I think is real, was real? It is also possible that there will be a real attack. It may have happened. It may be the truth of my life that I have lost everything — and at that time I was dreaming this while sleeping. It is worth thinking about.'"*

> *"Do you have any concrete proof, any evidence, that the life you are living is not a dream? Who knows — the dream you are seeing is your waking state. Who knows — you may be sleeping somewhere right now and dreaming while sleeping. Which discourse are you listening to?"*

**On religion and the one God:**
> *"What is the fight about? There is only one God in everyone's heart. Give him any form, any name. He will remain the same. Whoever is happy by saying Allah, let him call himself Allah. Whoever is happy by saying Hari, let him call himself Hari. Whoever is happy by saying Jesus Christ, let him call himself Jesus Christ. Whoever is happy by saying Guru Nanak, let him call himself Guru Nanak. There has never been a single religion in the history of human life, nor will there ever be."*

**On giving as the law of nature:**
> *"Look at the law of nature. If you want to take anything, you have to give first. If you give a seed to the earth, you will get a child… everything has to be given first, then you get something. Learn to give. I am not talking about giving money only — give love, give feelings, give faith, give devotion. Speak sweet words. Give happiness to someone's mind. Bring happiness in someone's life. If someone comes to your house, at least feed him. If you cannot feed him, at least give him water. If you cannot give him water, at least ask him to sit. If you cannot do even that, at least ask him to come inside."*

**On vanity and the brevity of life:**
> *"You have spent 40 years of your life — do you know what is its essence? You ate every day, you slept every day, you enjoyed sometimes, you acquired knowledge, you earned money. Apart from this, what did you do? Look at yourself and know yourself. You spent 40 years in these four-five things. Why? Who told you? Maybe no one told you — but you yourself did not think about it, did not know it, did not consider it. You just kept running, running, running like ants, like flies, like bees, just kept collecting."*

**Closing chant:**
> *Om Purnamadah Purnamidam Purnaat Purnamudachyate / Purnasya Purnamaadaaya Purnamevaavashishyate.*
> *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare.*
> *Hari Om Tat Sat. Hari Om Tat Sat. Hari Om Tat Sat.*

*Tone: He opens with the Ganesha Vakratunda shloka and a salutation to "the divine within you." His Hindi is conversational, peppered with rhetorical questions ("क्या आपके पास कोई प्रमाण है?"). He uses long descriptive stories (King Janaka takes most of the talk) and lets the moral land at the end. Closes every Bhaj Govindam talk with the Shanti Mantra + Maha Mantra + "Hari Om Tat Sat" thrice.*

### 8. Purity — Physical, Mental, and Moral

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zcs97j3oK9s
- **Channel:** Om Swami TV
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — English

**Opening:**
> *"First of all, I offer my obeisance to the divine in you."*

**The central teaching — purity is the precondition for everything spiritual:**
> *"Pretty much in all the scriptures there is a very important word — a word without which your penance, your sadhana, your practices are going to be incomplete. The word is **purity**. Whatever you do on the path of self-realization, you cannot discount purity… Without a pure mind, purity of your discipline, it is not possible to rise above intellect. Until such time that you rise above intellect, you are not going to experience your true nature."*

**The four levels of purity, mapped to Patanjali's eight limbs:**
1. **Moral purity** → *yama* (non-stealing, non-violence, truthfulness, celibacy)
2. **Mental purity** → *niyama* (study of scriptures, etc.)
3. **Physical purity** → *asana*
4. *Pranayama* — "purifying the vehicle of meditation"
5. *Pratyahara* (withdrawal of senses) → "that is spiritual purity. You cannot withdraw your senses from contemplating on material objects until you have purified yourself at the four levels."

**The mirror analogy:**
> *"Imagine you buy a really expensive mirror. Let's say you spend $10,000 buying it. The mirror is exquisite — it renders images perfectly. Now envision that you cover that mirror with some dust, with clothes, with something that makes it opaque… You have the most expensive mirror, but it is not solving the purpose, because it is unable to render images. And same is the state of your mind. Unless you have a pure mind, you cannot really attain that supreme state of meditation."*

**The water analogy:**
> *"Imagine a glass of water. The water in the glass is salty, and your job is to purify it. You have a couple of options. One is to keep pouring fresh water in… there will be a time when there will be no salty water left. Or — how about evaporating? How about the process of distillation? Could that remove impurities? The answer is yes. However, until you really, in a disciplined fashion, do the practice of purifying the water, the water is not going to be purified. You can have a glass full of water in front of you and chant all the mantras on the planet that talk about purifying water — that is not going to purify it, is it?"*

**The unclean cloth — purity of method matters:**
> *"Imagine a piece of cloth that is soiled. Can you clean a crystal bowl with that unclean cloth? Chances are, if you start cleaning that crystal bowl with that cloth, you're going to actually make it more dirty. And that's what I mean. If you are going to attempt even to try cleansing your mind or your soul with views or processes or discipline that is not pure at the first place — how on earth can you expect to perform the cleansing?"*

**The story of the professor and the overflowing teacup** (a classic Zen tale, in Swami ji's telling):
> *"There was a guy, very well read. A PhD, a professor in a state university. He hears about this monk who had recently come to town. So one fine day he decides to go to the monk. He goes there, and with complete disregard to the basic mannerisms of how you approach somebody you want to learn from — certainly from the eastern point of view — he just goes there, takes his seat and says, 'Please give me some knowledge. Give me some jnana.' The monk says, 'Oh sure. What is the rush? Why don't you have a cup of tea first?' The monk makes tea, gives the professor an empty cup, and starts pouring. And he keeps on pouring. And the cup is full now. But he keeps on pouring. The hot tea starts to overflow and gets onto the hands of the professor, who shouts out: 'Oh my god, can't you see the cup is full?! I'm done! I don't want more tea!' And the monk goes: **'That's exactly what I want you to know. If you are going to come full — how can I give you any more?'**"*

> *"So mental purification is about clearing up and cleansing your mind from all the silly things you have learned over the years from half-cooked sources, half-baked knowledge that you have not put to experience. Knowledge you have just accepted."*

**On signs of self-realization:**
> *"When the only thing you give out is love, you are self-realized. When the only thing that you can do under all circumstances, disagreeable circumstances — I mean, when the only thing you do is forgive — you are self-realized. Because these are the signs of a self-realized person. They have limitless compassion, generosity, forgiveness."*

**Closing:**
> *"In summary, purification — mental, physical, intellectual, moral — will lead to your self-purification. When you stand self-purified, you are ready to do self-analysis. That will lead you to the path of self-discovery, and that will take you further to the path of self-realization. This is the way to go, my friend. If you liked what I said, you are free to keep it. Else you're free to just discard it. Thank you very much. That's all I have for you today."*

*Tone: Calm, repetitive (he himself warns "the word purity is going to come many times today — be prepared"). He builds with everyday analogies (mirror, salty water, soiled cloth, tea cup) before naming the Sanskrit framework. Always closes with "That's all I have for you today" + the disciple's-choice flourish: "you are free to keep it or discard it."*

### 9. Relationships

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UFj4Z5OiLC8
- **Channel:** Om Swami TV
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — English

**Opening (signature):**
> *"I offer my obeisance to the Divine in you. I'm going to cover an important topic today. It's about relationships."*

**The framing — bond or transaction?**
> *"What is a relationship? Is it a transaction? Is relationship a transaction of convenience, of needs, of desires — or is it a bond? A bond of love? Does a relationship come about because there is a bond, or because there is a need? When a relationship is born out of need, clearly it is a selfish relationship, and may just wither away when needs are fulfilled. When the relationship is the result of a bond, it tends to last longer."*

**Love is not a transaction:**
> *"Love is independent. If nature loves you, it gives you rain regardless. It gives you food regardless. It is not saying to you, 'If you don't love me, I am not going to give you what I used to give you.' And that is the fundamental difference between love and a transaction."*

> *"Love is not a give-and-take proposition. Love is actually about **giving** — giving love, giving freedom, giving that person space. When you give something out, let's say it's love, you may not get love back from the same person. Why? Because love is not a reciprocal arrangement with an individual — it is with God, it is with nature. So if you are giving out love, nature will most definitely give you love back. If you are giving love to your spouse, they may not be the one giving you love back directly — but you may get it in multiples from your children or from other people."*

**Expectations are bondage:**
> *"When you desire an outcome — when you want your partner to reciprocate in kind — you have just created bondage for yourself. Now your happiness is dependent on the reaction or the response from somebody, from the other person. You've just created dependency on something external. And there starts the beginning of non-fulfillment."*

**Why you cannot be filled from outside:**
> *"You want things from the external world because you have not started tapping into your inner world. Your communication with the inner world is actually broken down — that is why you turn outward for everything: joy, happiness, love, satisfaction, gratification. And because everything outside is scarce, interdependent and conditional, therefore you're always having to compete. That exercise is like ranging the universe from one end to the other. It is a never-ending exercise."*

**Hard truth about loveless relationships:**
> *"You can be married to somebody for 40 years, for 30 years, for 50 years, and still feel that sense of non-fulfillment. Why? Because all these years, chances are, you actually did not have a bond as such. You merely curbed your expectations… and because you put a lid on what you wanted, you may have projected to the outside world as a happy couple — but deep within, you are not happy. Because nobody can be happy by curbing their desires. You have to **understand** the nature of the desires."*

**The dog analogy — unconditional love:**
> *"If you have a pet and you love that pet — the pet communicates with you only in a very confined manner, but you still love that pet. Your dog is not going out there 9-to-5 earning money for you or paying the bills, but you don't hate your dog for that. Why? Because you have accepted it. But when it comes to other human beings in the family, suddenly your expectations go through the roof. You may have a ton of very good excuses or justifications — but you are not going to have any reason for it. **Why? Because love is beyond reason.**"*

**The hard mirror — "it is you who needs fixing":**
> *"Sometimes people go through a divorce, and when that person gets into a new relationship, the new partner is a happier person. It is not uncommon to hear that the person they have now married is actually quite happy. But you are not. And you may have married another person but you're still not happy. **What does that tell you?**"*

> *"If you are not happy, I'm sorry to tell you this, but it is your problem. It is **you** who needs fixing. It is **you** who needs to make a trip to the garage and get serviced. Because your non-happiness is due to your own state of mind."*

**Summary (his five points):**
> *"In summary: (1) You cannot make a relationship good by negating your desires. (2) You cannot change the other person. (3) Your state of happiness is your creation and entirely dependent on you. (4) Moving from one relationship to another may offer temporary comfort but eventually it will be like your present relationship. (5) You need to turn inward to discover your source of peace."*

*Tone: This is one of his most direct, no-nonsense talks. He uses the word "my friend" affectionately, but the message itself is uncompromising. The dog analogy lands hard and is classic Swami ji — a homely, slightly humorous image that turns into a philosophical knife. He gives the listener "the right to disagree totally" but then asks them to think about it.*

### 10. What Do You Want

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mBdejmXp4KE
- **Channel:** Om Swami TV
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — English

**Opening:**
> *"I offer my obeisance to the Divine in you. Generally speaking, everybody wants something either in their life or from their life. If I ask you, what do you want? Do you know the answer to that question?"*

**The supermarket analogy — clarity precedes manifestation:**
> *"Imagine you walk into a supermarket. You have the money with you, and you even have the list of things you want to buy. Your life becomes easy — all you have to do is go around the aisles and pick up the items and then check out. But imagine you walk into the same shop and you don't know what you want. How are you actually going to make any choices at all? You are relying on the suggestions given to you by other people."*

**The restaurant — and the indecisive customer:**
> *"You walk into a restaurant. The waiter comes to you: 'What would you like for breakfast?' You say, 'I would love some brown bread, hash browns and a cup of coffee.' A few minutes later you call him again — 'Actually, can you cancel that order, bring me pancakes.' He goes inside, places the new order. A few minutes go by, you call him again: 'Look, I'm really sorry to be a pain, but can you change my order one last time? Can you just give me fruit yogurt with French toast?' Once in a while this may be okay — but imagine doing it **every day**. You do the math. **What is the likelihood that next time you will be taken seriously?**"*

> *"And the same goes with God — with nature, whatever name you want to give to Providence. They are there to give you. God is there to help you. Nature is there to serve you. But if you're going to keep changing the order, you're going to have problems. Nature one day is going to stop taking you seriously."*

**On dilution of intent:**
> *"Once you have released a thought in the cosmos — 'I would like this' — if that is the only thing you want, you will have that. Not in your time frame; in nature's time frame. But if you want other things as well, you have **diluted the strength, the concentration of your thought**. The more you want, the weaker the thought. The more you add to it, the more feeble it becomes."*

**The signature line — patience and patience:**
> *"There's a saying they use very frequently in Australia — that's where I first heard it — **'You cannot make nine ladies pregnant and deliver the baby in a month.'** You've got to wait."*

**The well-digger:**
> *"Imagine you start digging a well because you want water. You go down 10 feet and you're tired. You say, 'Probably there's no water in this land,' and you go to another place and dig another 10 feet, and another, and another. You can do this all your life, but you're not going to get water. If you want water from the ground, you have to sit at one place and **keep digging**. Of course, you need to be smart about selection of the place at the first place. But you need to keep digging. Once you go down 60 feet, 80 feet, 90 feet — chances are you're going to get water."*

**On goals — be your own author:**
> *"How can I tell you what should be your life's goal? It is your life. If I give you a goal, that means I have just conditioned your mind — and I'm so against conditioning the mind. The whole point is to **remove** the conditioning of the mind. So you need to set your own goal."*

> *"If your goal is to have a company bigger than Microsoft — that is a rubbish goal, believe me. You can aim to be the industry leader, to be creative, to have your own business, to have a better quality product than your competitor. But if your goal is to have a bigger company than somebody else, the only way you can do it is to go back in time and start it when the other person started it, with the same market conditions."*

**Priorities — you can't have it all at once:**
> *"You can't eat the cake and have it too — unless you can make your own dough and make another cake at home. That is possible. And for that to happen you need to have **razor-sharp focus**. If you have supreme concentration and excellent ability to switch off, you can have both. If you can be fully at work when at work and be fully at home when at home, you can have both family and work. But if you want to work 80 hours a week and spend 10 hours at the gym and meditate for 4 hours and spend 20 hours with the family — and also have decent sleep of 6 to 8 hours every night — I don't think it's going to happen. Do you even have those many hours in the day?"*

**On peace as a worthy goal:**
> *"If you ask me what should you want, I would say one of the things should be **peace**. Because you can only be peaceful if you're contented. When your head is in the right spot, you can only be peaceful. The struggle that is unnecessary leaves you."*

**Closing:**
> *"Know what you want, and nature will help you get there. If you don't know what you want — either don't complain when you get anything, or be happy with whatever you get. When you don't specify what you want, you are out there in the open market and may go for just about any price. Delve deep. Turn inward. Discover yourselves. Think about it. That's all I have for you today. Om Krishna Krishna. Hari, Hari, Hari, Hari. Om."*

*Tone: This talk is a masterclass in his everyday-life pedagogy. Three concrete analogies (supermarket, restaurant, well-digger) carry almost the entire teaching. The "nine ladies pregnant" line is pure Swami ji — folksy, slightly cheeky, instantly memorable. Notice how he never says "the cosmos" or "manifestation" without grounding them in something kitchen-table simple.*

### 11. Tantra Sadhana App Launch — Enter the World of the Das Mahavidya with Om Swami

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p8B2ooEJh3Y
- **Channel:** Tantra Talks Official (`@Tantratalksofficial`)
- **Speaker:** **Om Swami ji** (live launch, May 2025 era)
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — English, very long (~10,000 words). The selections below preserve the *teaching* portions; the app-walkthrough sections are summarized in italics.

**Opening invocation (verbatim):**
> *"Um, Jai Sher [Sri Mata]… Sri Sundarya Sindhu… All glories to divine mother. First of all, I offer my obeisance to the divine in you, with whose grace we're all here right now this moment."*

**On the misconceptions around Tantra:**
> *"There are many, many misconceptions about tantra in the world out there… You can differentiate between the shat-karma in tantra — that is **maaran, uchchatan, vashikaran, sammohan, vidveshan, stambhan** — and the real tantra. The so-called Vamachara practices because of which tantra has gained a notorious kind of reputation in many parts of the world."*

**Origins of Tantra — pre-Vedic, from Lord Shiva:**
> *"Tantric practices actually existed even **before the Vedic era**. The tribes of Rudra — Lord Shiva — were the first ones to propagate tantra. In fact, all of the scriptures that he has spoken are called **Agama**. Agama is where Ma — the mother goddess — asks a question and Shiva answers. **Nigama** is where the reverse happens, where Mother is answering the question and Lord Shiva is asking. So they existed before even the Vedic practices were there."*

> *"In the beginning we only had three Vedas — Rig, Sama, Yajur. There was no Atharva, because Atharva at that time was transmitted orally. All the practices of tantra as we know today — what we know today has been polluted to a great degree — but those practices emerged from Atharva. It was **Parashara Rishi** who asked Vyasa to get Atharva included as one of the main Vedas, and due to his influence at that time he was somebody who could get that done."*

**The five schools of Tantra:**
> *"Tantric practices that emerged at the time had **five different schools**:
> - **Dakshinachar** (the right-handed path)
> - **Vamachar** (the left-handed path)
> - **Kaulachar**
> - **Samayachar**
> - **Mishrachar** (the mixed path, which emerged much later and was taken up particularly in Bihar, Uttar Pradesh and West Bengal)."*

**How Tantra was distorted — the John Woodroffe and Pierre Bernard story:**
> *"About 150 years ago two significant events happened. One was **Sir John Woodroffe**, a magistrate and judge in India — a British man — who, under the pen-name **Arthur Avalon**, wrote *The Serpent Power* and *Shakti and Shakta*. He took Tantra to the West and, quite remarkably, spoke about the pristine nature of Tantra as opposed to the popular tantric practices. Until then, in the West, tantra was seen as hedonistic, an obscure set of occult practices."*

> *"Around 1905 there was another American occultist — **Pierre Bernard** I think — who was mostly interested in the sexual aspect of tantra. Now really, there was no sexual aspect as such. There was the concept of **panchamakara**: **matsya** (fish), **madira** (wine), **mamsa** (meat), **mudra** (handlocks, parched grains, or bodily poses) and **maithuna**. The Americans took a shine to this and eventually took out all the rituals — no chanting, no invocation, no divinity — and it became only about sexual and sensual experiences."*

**The Tibetan transmission and how Mahachina-krama was born:**
> *"Around 1500 years ago, our scriptures went overseas. Buddhist monks were the first to take them, notably to Tibet. There it got mixed with what they called the **Bön religion** — one of the original shamanic religions of Tibet. They used to worship a goddess called **Tara**. From there emerged a practice called **Mahachina-krama** — the original name was **Mahachina-Tara-krama** — which involved a female and a male. This became very popular. So when those shamanic practices came back to India, in all the states sharing a border with Tibet — Sikkim, Kashmir, Himachal Pradesh, Arunachal Pradesh — tantra took on a different form."*

**Why fearlessness is essential, and why he tells the truth even when it's unpopular:**
> *"My job is to put the truth out there for you, and the rest is up to you — to believe or not to believe, to take it or leave it. That's entirely your call. It's not my prerogative. It's yours."*

**The Dasha Mahavidyas, listed by his own hand:**

**The full Sri Vidya path, and why he refuses shortcuts:**

> *"Could I have hastened the process? - *On stopping alcohol or smoking for sadhana:* **"Absolutely no need to stop anything from a sadhana perspective. Of course it's good not to drink or smoke for your health — but not from a sadhana perspective. You can start any day."**

**Closing:**

*Tone: This is Swami ji at the lectern as a scholar-tantric. He calls Tantra "the highest form of worship" not as boast but as encouragement: anyone can do it. The closing — "Can a mother ever harm you?" — is the one line you most want to remember from this entire batch.*

### 12. Bhakti vs Tantra — The Journey from Devotion to Divinity

- **Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K2rGI9CaHNM
- **Channel:** Tantra Talks Official
- **Speaker:** **Om Swami ji**
- **Type:** `[auto-captions-cleaned]` — English, full short transcript

**The full teaching (verbatim, very short and pithy):**

> *"What is the difference between **bhakti** and **tantra**? Tantra is a form of bhakti. Tantra is a type of bhakti."*

> *"In **bhakti** you believe: I am the embodied soul, and He — or She, which is just a matter of saying, there is no difference — is the Supreme Soul. There is no match between the two. I will forever remain polluted, and He, or Goddess She, will forever remain unpolluted — most pure — **paramshuddha**, most awake and most pure, **paramabuddha**, **nitya** — forever pure, forever awake, enlightened, forever pure. Just like you cannot dissolve a rock in milk or water, bhakti says: **I am too impure to be dissolved. So I am going to serve God.**"*

> *"Bhakti is purely about service. I am going to serve, and I'm going to serve as a friend. I'm going to serve as a servant. I'm going to serve like a servant would serve his or her master. That's bhakti in a nutshell. True bhakti will always keep you humble, will keep you grounded, will keep you centered, will keep you real. It would give you peace, because you believe: **I don't have to do everything myself. There is a Master who is looking after me.**"*

> *"**Tantra**, on the other hand, says: no question about it that You are supreme, that You are very superior, that You are very special — but I am **made from You**. I am a part of You. If I am a part of You, then it must be possible, by definition, for me to rise to such a level of purity someday, some point of time in my life, when I will be able to **merge in You**."*

> *"But — I am not interested in merging after death. I don't care about any liberation, salvation, any moksha, any nirvana **after I am dead**. I very much would like it when I am alive. Therefore, I am going to keep calling out to You — rising above my base fears, my primal fears and primal emotions of ignorance, lust, hatred, jealousy, envy, ego, anger, and so on. I'm going to keep purifying myself and keep calling out to You till one such day that **You show Yourself to me. And the moment I see You — I will become You.**"*

> *"That's the fundamental difference between bhakti and tantra."*

*Tone: This may be the single most concise and powerful definition of Tantra in the entire corpus. He gives bhakti its full dignity first — "true bhakti will always keep you humble" — and then introduces Tantra not as a rebellion against bhakti, but as a more impatient form of the same love: "I don't want this after I am dead. I want this now." The repeated rhythm "I'm going to keep purifying myself and keep calling out to You" lands like a vow.*
