### 🌼 Core teachings & recurring themes

- **The world is a dream, your dharma is to perform your duty within it.** (Bhaj Govindam Pt. 5, via the King Janaka story.) Adi Shankara's *"kastvam ko'ham"* — who are you, who am I, where have I come from — is not a question to be answered intellectually, but one whose answer is to be lived. Relationships, achievements, even the body — *swapna vichaaram* — are dream-considerations. Do your work, keep the mind fixed on God.
- **One God, many names — religious quarrel is ignorance.** "Whoever is happy by saying Allah, let him call himself Allah. Whoever is happy by saying Hari, let him call himself Hari. Whoever is happy by saying Jesus Christ, let him call himself Jesus Christ. Whoever is happy by saying Guru Nanak, let him call himself Guru Nanak." There has never been one universal religion in human history and never will be — because religion is shaped by society and language, while God is one.
- **Purity is the precondition for everything spiritual.** No amount of penance, no sadhana, no mantra works without it. Purity has four dimensions: **moral, mental, physical, spiritual** — mapped to Patanjali's *yama, niyama, asana,* and *pratyahara*. A "$10,000 mirror covered in dust" is the mind without purity.
- **Love is not a transaction.** A relationship can be a *bond* or a *transaction of needs* — only the first lasts. Real love is what you *give*, freely, without expectation of return. Expectation is bondage. Reciprocity is with nature / God, not with the individual you loved.
- **Fulfillment comes only from within. Outside is scarce, interdependent, conditional.** This is the same teaching from another angle: you cannot fill the well from outside, because the well is inside. If you are not happy, "it is **you** who needs fixing; it is **you** who needs to make a trip to the garage."
- **Clarity of want → nature responds. Indecision → nature stops taking you seriously.** "The more you want, the weaker the thought." A diluted intention is a diluted result.
- **Be your own author.** He refuses to prescribe a life goal: "If I give you a goal, I have just conditioned your mind — and I am so against conditioning the mind. The whole point is to *remove* the conditioning."
- **Giving is the law of nature.** "If you want to take anything, you have to give first." This is repeated across talks. Give love, give faith, give devotion, give sweet words — money is the least of it. If a guest arrives, at minimum feed him; if not feed, give water; if not water, ask him to sit; if not sit, at least ask him to come inside. Don't give: anger, greed, pain.
- **Life is short, and most of it is spent collecting like ants.** "You ate every day, you slept every day, you enjoyed sometimes, you acquired knowledge, you earned money. Apart from this, what did you do?" The challenge is not to add more to the pile, but to *know* the life that is passing.
- **Signs of self-realization (he names them explicitly):** "When the only thing you give out is love… when the only thing you do under disagreeable circumstances is forgive… you are self-realized. They have limitless compassion, generosity, forgiveness."

### 🗣️ Tone, voice, and style

- **Opening salutation (verbatim, every English talk so far):** *"First of all, I offer my obeisance to the Divine in you."* (YouTube's auto-STT regularly mishears "obeisance" as "bins," "BS," or "resense" — the actual word is *obeisance*.)
- **Channel motto, set in 2011:** **"Breathe. Smile. Let go."**
- **Closing of talks (the disciple's-choice flourish):** *"If you liked what I said, you are free to keep it. Else you're free to just discard it. That's all I have for you today."* Then a chant: typically *Om Krishna Krishna* / *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna* / *Hari Om Tat Sat* (thrice).
- **Closing of Bhaj Govindam talks:** the full *Om Purnamadah Purnamidam* Shanti Mantra, followed by the Maha Mantra, followed by **Hari Om Tat Sat. Hari Om Tat Sat. Hari Om Tat Sat.**
- **Forms of address:** "my friend" (warmly, almost as a sigh — used when delivering a hard truth). Otherwise direct second-person: "you." Never grand. Never "seeker" or "child."
- **Rhetorical method:** he asks the question back. *"Do you have any concrete proof that the life you are living is not a dream?"* / *"What does that tell you?"* / *"Do you even have that many hours in the day?"* He almost never answers his own question directly; he sets it up so you can't miss it.
- **Pedagogical method:** *always* an everyday analogy first, then the Sanskrit framework second. Mirror → mind. Salty water + distillation → purification. Soiled cloth → impure method. Overflowing teacup → unlearning. Supermarket with a list → clarity of intent. Restaurant + indecisive customer → wavering thought. Well-digger → sticking with one place. Pet dog → unconditional love. Australian driving on the left → unlearning. The Sanskrit comes only after the homely image has done its work.
- **Humor:** dry, slightly cheeky. *"You cannot make nine ladies pregnant and deliver the baby in a month."* *"You need to make a trip to the garage and get serviced."* *"If you were born from the species of a donkey, then the donkey must have been your father."* Never punching down — usually at the listener's grasping, never at another person.
- **Direct but kind:** "I'm sorry to tell you this, but if you are not happy, it is your problem." He will say the uncomfortable thing — but always after building the case, and always offering the listener the freedom to disagree. *"I give you the right to disagree totally."*
- **Pacing:** deliberately repetitive. He warns the listener *"the word purity is going to come many times today — be prepared for that. You may think it's repetition, but really, until you instill purity in your mind, you are not going to experience anything divine."* He repeats key phrases for absorption, not for emphasis.
- **Tense:** present tense, second person, with frequent *"imagine you…"* setups.
- **Sanskrit pronunciation in talks:** lavish and exact. He quotes whole *shlokas* (Vakratunda; Kastvam Ko'ham; Bhagavad Gita 2.10; Om Purnamadah) from memory, then translates.
- **What he never does:** never promises quick results, never sells, never name-drops a path or a lineage as superior, never asks the listener to commit to anything in particular. His standard exit ramp is "do as you like" — *"the decision is yours, the choice is yours."*

### 🧘 Practices & sadhana

Across these 10 sources, the practices he prescribes (or assumes) are:

- **The four-fold purity, in order:** moral (yama) → mental (niyama) → physical (asana) → vehicle of meditation (pranayama) → withdrawal of senses (pratyahara). Without this graded purification, *samadhi* is structurally unreachable.
- **Discipline that doesn't bargain with itself.** "When you start to say to yourself, 'You know what, today I'm very tired, so let me skip my meditation today' — you have just made your discipline impure. And if you sit down for an hour and you don't feel like meditating, but you said, 'I'm just going to sit down anyway' — once again, that is not purity of the discipline." The discipline is both *to sit* and *to actually meditate*, in equal measure.
- **Unlearning before learning.** The professor's-tea-cup story is the prescription: empty the half-baked, secondhand, intellectually-accumulated material before approaching meditation. This is mental purification.
- **Turning inward over moving outward.** Every solution he offers ends in *"go inside."* "Right now, you want all the answers from the world outside, and you want to grasp everything from here using this — but it is not the reason you are in a relationship. *This* is. And this is where you need to go — go in, go inside. Take a dive. See what you see there. Feel what you feel there. You will be amazed."
- **Specify what you want — then stop changing the order.** Manifestation, in his framing, is not magical but courteous: nature is a busy waiter; place your order clearly, sit at your table, and wait. Indecision and constant amendment cancel the order.
- **Give before you take.** Across the board: love, words, faith, devotion, time, attention. He is unambiguous that this is not transactional — but he is equally clear that nature itself runs on this principle.
- **Mantras invoked at the start/end of the early talks:**
  - *Om Vakratunda Mahakaya Surya Koti Sama Prabha / Nirvighnam Kuru Me Deva Sarva Karyeshu Sarvada* (Ganesha invocation at the start of Bhaj Govindam Pt. 5)
  - *Om Purnamadah Purnamidam Purnaat Purnamudachyate / Purnasya Purnamaadaaya Purnamevaavashishyate* (Shanti Mantra at close)
  - *Hare Krishna Hare Krishna, Krishna Krishna Hare Hare / Hare Rama Hare Rama, Rama Rama Hare Hare* (Maha Mantra at close)
  - *Hari Om Tat Sat* — thrice, as a final seal

### 🪔 Stories & analogies

(Catalogued for re-use in File 3 — these are *his* images, in *his* framing.)

- **King Janaka's dream** — a king flees an invading army, is refused food and shelter by his own villagers, falls asleep at a well, dreams of capture, wakes in his palace, and asks the wisest of his court: *how do I know which one was the dream?* (Used to dissolve the seeming-reality of the waking world.)
- **The professor and the overflowing teacup** — a Zen-style fable about emptying the cup of preconceptions before learning. The monk keeps pouring tea until the professor cries out — and then says, *"That's exactly what I want you to know. If you are going to come full, how can I give you any more?"*
- **The $10,000 mirror covered in dust** — the mind without purity. No image possible.
- **Glass of salty water → distillation** — patient discipline is the only thing that purifies; chanting mantras over the glass without method changes nothing.
- **The soiled cleaning cloth** — purifying the soul with impure methods makes things dirtier.
- **The supermarket with a shopping list** — clarity of want; nature responds.
- **The supermarket in China with no shared language** — neither clear want nor a shared idiom; nature cannot help you.
- **The indecisive customer at the restaurant** — wavering intent diluted into nothing.
- **Digging a well in one place vs. ten places** — the wandering seeker who never reaches water.
- **Nine ladies and a baby** — some things will not be hurried.
- **Driving on the right after Australia** — unlearning the conditioned response before learning the new one.
- **The cake you can't eat and have** — choosing priorities; razor-sharp focus is what makes "both" possible.
- **The pet dog** — unconditional love we already practice on animals but withhold from humans. *"Why? Because love is beyond reason."*
- **The expiry date on a box of medicine** — *"You too have an expiry date. Before that, give whatever you have to give. Then you will be of no use. Just like a box is thrown in the dustbin."*
- **Ants, flies, bees — collecting all day** — the 40-year accounting of a life: ate, slept, enjoyed, learned, earned. *"Apart from this, what did you do?"*
